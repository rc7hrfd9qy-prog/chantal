About

Writer, seeker,
village dweller

Born in Halifax, Nova Scotia, and raised in the suburbs of Montreal, Chantal Martineau has spent more than two decades writing about wine and spirits, food, and culture. Her work is rooted in a deep curiosity about people, place, and the stories that live inside a glass — stories she has pursued from the agave fields of Oaxaca to the speakeasies of New York City.

She is the author of How the Gringos Stole Tequila (Trinity University Press, 2015) and co-author, with mezcal pioneer Ron Cooper (founder of Del Maguey), of Finding Mezcal: A Journey into the Liquid Soul of Mexico (Ten Speed Press / Penguin Random House, 2018). Her first book was adapted into the acclaimed documentary Agave: The Spirit of a Nation, which premiered at South by Southwest in 2018.

Her bylines span Vogue, The Atlantic, Food & Wine, Bon Appétit, Saveur, The Guardian, The Washington Post, Forbes, The Financial Times, and many more. She is also a poet — her work appears in Understorey Magazine — and a potter.

She is currently at work on her first novel, inspired by her grandfather's research into their African Nova Scotian family tree. She lives in a small village in the Hudson Valley with her partner and two daughters.

How the Gringos Stole Tequila

An investigation into one of the world's most popular spirits — and the battle between American appetite and Mexican cultural identity. Martineau spent years traveling to distilleries and agave farms in Mexico, interviewing mixologists, academics, and farmers on both sides of the border. Finding Mezcal

Co-authored with Ron Cooper, founder of Del Maguey Single Village Mezcal, this is a celebration of Mexico's most soulful spirit. Documentary Film — Screenwriter

Agave: The Spirit of a Nation

World Premiere • SXSW 2018 • Directed by Nicholas Kovacic & Matthew Riggieri • Narrated by Damián Alcázar

Based on her groundbreaking book, Martineau served as screenwriter for this 85-minute documentary that takes viewers beyond the spirits of mezcal and tequila to discover how one delicate plant can carry the weight of an entire nation. The film follows three family producers — Graciela Angeles, Aquilino García López, and Carlos Camarena — from the bio-diverse landscapes of Jalisco to Oaxaca.

The film also premiered at the Festival Internacional de Cine in Guadalajara, Mexico.

Speaking & Services

Bringing stories
to life in person

Chantal Martineau is available for speaking engagements, workshops, and consulting projects that sit at the intersection of drinks, culture, and storytelling.

Speaking Engagements

Talks and presentations on tequila, mezcal, and agave spirits — their history, cultural significance, and the people fighting to protect them. Also covers the craft of food and drink journalism, and the stories that live inside a glass.

Past venues include the Culinary Historians of New York, Heritage Radio Network, and virtual events for Hudson Valley arts organizations.

Cocktail & Spirits Tours

Chantal has guided walking tours through New York City's cocktail history for Context Travel — visiting iconic hotel bars, storied speakeasies, and rare library collections, culminating in a guided tasting. Private mezcal and tequila education events are also available for small groups.

Editorial Consulting

With two decades of experience across major publications, Chantal offers editorial consulting for brands, publishers, and media companies working in food, beverage, and culture.

Writing & Journalism

Available for commissioned features, profiles, essays, and longform storytelling. Specialties include wine and spirits, travel, food culture, and narrative nonfiction.
