About

Writer
and author

Born in Halifax, Nova Scotia, and raised in the suburbs of Montreal, Chantal Martineau has spent more than two decades writing about wine and spirits, food, and culture. Her work is driven by curiosity — about people, place, and the traditions that shape what ends up on the table.

She is the author of How the Gringos Stole Tequila (Trinity University Press, 2015) and co-author, with Ron Cooper, of Finding Mezcal: A Journey into the Liquid Soul of Mexico (Ten Speed Press / Penguin Random House, 2018). Her first book was the basis for the documentary Agave: The Spirit of a Nation, which premiered at SXSW in 2018.

Her writing has appeared in Vogue, The Atlantic, Food & Wine, Bon Appétit, Saveur, The Guardian, The Washington Post, Forbes, The Financial Times, and many others. She is also a poet and a potter.

She lives in a small village in the Hudson Valley with her partner and two daughters.

An investigation into one of the world's most popular spirits — and what happens when a tradition becomes a commodity. Martineau spent years traveling to Mexico and interviewing distillers, farmers, academics, and mixologists on both sides of the border to understand what is at stake.
